**Status:** Approved

**Source:** `docs/cb-connect-ideation-and-product-gap-analysis-2026-07-17.md`

## Decision

CB Connect v0.2.0 will be planned as a trust-first release that validates a
small Care Loop workflow. The milestone will not attempt to implement every
idea in the source analysis.

The documentation will distinguish three levels of confidence:

- **Committed:** trust and lifecycle corrections required before new sensitive
  workflows;
- **Candidate:** the smallest Care Loop slice suitable for product validation;
- **Deferred:** mobile, health import, learned care preferences, and broader
  tracking until prerequisite evidence exists.

This avoids presenting product hypotheses as proven differentiation or
architecture options as committed roadmap items.

## Product scope

### Gate 0: trust foundation

The v0.2.0 plan must address known authorization and lifecycle risks before
Care Loop implementation:

- canonical Clerk-to-Convex identity mapping;
- stable relationship membership and role invariants;
- partner leave, primary revoke, unlink, relink, and account-deletion behavior;
- non-destructive per-user chat hiding instead of unilateral global deletion;
- pairing-code, date-validation, and notification-idempotency issues;
- auditable consent, revocation propagation, and safe notification previews.

### Gate 1: Care Loop validation slice

The first Care Loop candidate includes:

- one active, expiring Care Card per primary user;
- request-only sharing as the safest default;
- up to three helpful actions and three avoid actions;
- partner responses limited to acknowledgement or inability to help;
- primary-user cancellation and immediate revocation;
- immutable partner-visible share snapshots;
- complete actor attribution, authorization checks, and audit events.

Selected health-detail sharing, accept/done workflows, outcome learning,
weekly recaps, preference graphs, expanded symptom capture, and bounded chat
clarifications are deferred until the validation slice produces evidence.

## Architecture

Private check-in state and partner-visible state are separate. A mutable private
check-in must never be returned to a partner or serve as the consent receipt.
Publishing creates an immutable, recipient-specific share snapshot with an
explicit source revision, permitted fields, expiry, and revocation state.

Care actions and audit events form the state-transition record. Mutations must
derive the authenticated user server-side, enforce actor-specific transitions,
reject stale revisions, and accept idempotency keys for safe retries.

Expiry is enforced twice:

1. every partner read and action checks current authorization and expiry;
2. a scheduled internal transition updates reactive state at expiry time.

Notifications are downstream of committed domain events. Domain events,
in-app inbox items, delivery plans, delivery attempts, and provider receipts are
separate records. Authorization is rechecked immediately before external
delivery so cancellation, unlinking, or expiry cannot be bypassed by retries.

## Data and privacy rules

- Existing pain, phase, and assisted-period flags remain category defaults and
  upper bounds; they do not implicitly authorize Care Loop sharing.
- Request-only cards reveal no underlying pain, symptom, energy, or cycle data.
- Private notes are never copied into partner-visible snapshots or notification
  payloads.
- Partners cannot distinguish absent private data from withheld private data.
- Revocation immediately blocks reads, actions, and pending disclosure.
- Care events measure workflow health, never relationship quality or partner
  worth.
- Imported health data, if later added, remains private by default and is
  removable with its provenance.

## Error handling and lifecycle behavior

Specifications will define behavior for stale edits, duplicate offline retries,
expired cards, revoked relationships, unavailable partners, invalid transition
attempts, partial notification failures, and account deletion. User-facing
errors must be safe, specific, and must not confirm that private data exists.

## Validation and success criteria

Documentation is complete when:

- every canonical file declares status, milestone, dependencies, and owner;
- every work-ready spec includes invariants, state transitions, acceptance
  criteria, migration/backfill considerations, tests, telemetry, rollout, and
  rollback;
- repo claims match the refreshed `main` head and live GitHub state;
- market claims use direct public sources and avoid exclusivity claims that
  cannot be proved;
- all opaque source-system citation tokens are removed;
- links in the docs index resolve;
- a clean branch diff contains only intentional documentation changes;
- the branch is committed and pushed without merging or publishing v0.2.0.

## Alternatives considered

### Specify every ideation epic now

Rejected because it would turn mobile and health-import options into premature
commitments and create more authoritative-looking documents than the evidence
supports.

### Specify only Care Loop

Rejected because current identity, relationship lifecycle, shared deletion, and
notification behavior are prerequisites for safely adding the feature.

## Approved outcome

Produce a concise canonical product and architecture baseline, detailed Gate 0
and Care Loop specifications, a dependency-ordered v0.2.0 roadmap, and clearly
deferred decision records for later mobile and health-import work.
